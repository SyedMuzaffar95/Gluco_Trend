Model Pipeline

  • Features: {{ feature_names | join(", ") }}.
  • Target: HighGlucose = 1 if glucose ≥ {{ threshold }} mg/dL, else 0.
  • Train/test split: 80/20 with stratify, random_state=42.
  • Decision Tree: criterion="gini", max_depth=None.
  • Pruned Decision Tree: max_depth=4, min_samples_split=10, min_samples_leaf=5.
  • Random Forest: 100 trees, criterion="gini", max_depth=5.
  • AdaBoost: DecisionTree(max_depth=1) base, 50 estimators, learning_rate=1.0.
  • Gradient Boosting: 100 estimators, learning_rate=0.1, max_depth=3, subsample=0.8.
  • XGBoost: 100 trees, learning_rate=0.1, max_depth=3, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.8.
  • Metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, Confusion Matrix + Classification Report (printed to console).

Significance of These Models

  • Decision Tree — simple rule-based classifier. It splits the data using the most informative of the three features (glucose_lag_1, insulin_basal, carbs) and is highly interpretable. It is the building block on which all other models here are based.
  • Pruned Decision Tree — the unpruned tree grows until every leaf is pure and memorises noise (overfitting). Limiting depth to 4 and requiring min 10 samples per split / 5 per leaf keeps the tree simpler and more general on unseen glucose readings.
  • Random Forest — trains many decision trees on random data subsets and averages their votes (bagging). Averaging reduces variance and overfitting, which is why it usually performs better and more stably than a single tree on noisy physiological data.
  • AdaBoost — instead of voting, it trains trees sequentially, each one concentrating on the records the previous trees misclassified, giving more weight to hard cases (e.g. borderline readings around 140 mg/dL).
  • Gradient Boosting — builds trees sequentially where each new tree predicts the residual (error) of the current ensemble. Small depth-3 trees plus a low learning rate produce a strong, smooth decision boundary.
  • XGBoost — a highly optimised, regularised gradient boosting implementation. Its built-in L1/L2 regularisation, column sub-sampling and smart handling make it fast and robust, typically the strongest and most accurate model in this comparison.
